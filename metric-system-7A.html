Objective

Learn to multiply and divide decimals by powers of ten and to convert metric measurements.

You know that in a place-value chart each place value is ten times greater than the place value to its right.

The number of zeros in the power of ten, or the exponent in the power of ten, tells you how many places to move the decimal point.

Example 1: Multiplying and Dividing by Powers of Ten

Remember

Metric Units

The metric system uses powers of ten. The base unit for length is the meter, for mass the gram, and for capacity the liter. Prefixes are used to describe units that are greater or smaller than the base unit.

    Summary

    • The number of zeros in the power of ten (or its exponent) tells how many places to move the decimal point.
    • To multiply by a power of ten, move the decimal point to the right. To divide, move it to the left.
    • Base units: meter (length), gram (mass), liter (capacity). Kilo = thousand, centi = hundredth, milli = thousandth.
    • To convert to a smaller unit, multiply. To convert to a larger unit, divide.
    • Key facts: 1 km = 1 000 m, 1 m = 100 cm = 1 000 mm, 1 kg = 1 000 g, 1 ton = 1 000 kg, 1 L = 1 000 mL.
