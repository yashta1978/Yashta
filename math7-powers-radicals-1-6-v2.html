Key idea

A square is a power with the exponent 2. The square root of a number is one of its two equal factors. For a number that is not a perfect square, find the nearest perfect squares to estimate its square root.

This page explains each kind of question once. Exercises that repeat the same question are explained together, because the method is the same.

1. Vocabulary: perfect square, square root, radical sign Vocabulary 1, 2; page 26

The question: “What is the symbol √ called?” / “Write 36 as the square of a natural number.”

How to solve it:

  1. A perfect square is the square of a natural number.
  2. The square root of a number is one of its two equal factors.
  3. The symbol √ is called a radical sign.
Worked example (book). 36 = 6² and 6 is a natural number, so 36 is a perfect square. 4 × 4 = 16, so 4 is a square root of 16 and √16 = 4.

The core idea: a square root undoes a square.

2. Find the square of a number Example 1; exercises 1–4, 10–13, 24–27

The question: “Represent 7² by a square, then evaluate this power.”

How to solve it:

  1. Draw a square whose side is the base.
  2. area = side × side. Substitute the side length.
  3. Multiply. For a fraction, multiply the numerators and the denominators.
Worked example (book). 7² = 7 × 7 = 49 and 2.5² = 2.5 × 2.5 = 6.25.
Exercise 25. (3/8)² = 3/8 × 3/8 = 9/64.

The core idea: the exponent 2 means the number multiplied by itself.

3. Estimate a square root Example 2; exercises 5–8, 14–21, 28–31

The question: “Estimate √30 to the nearest whole number.”

How to solve it:

  1. Find the perfect squares nearest the number: 25 < 30 < 36.
  2. Find their square roots: 5 < √30 < 6.
  3. See which perfect square is closer: 30 is closer to 25, so √30 ≈ 5.
  4. Check with a calculator.
Worked example (book). √30 ≈ 5 and √99 ≈ 10.
Exercise 7. 81 < 84 < 100, so 9 < √84 < 10, and 84 is closer to 81: √84 ≈ 9.

The core idea: the nearest perfect squares trap the root between two whole numbers.

4. Word problems about a square area Example 3; exercises 9, 22, 23, 42, 43

The question: “A square has an area of 150 km². How long is each side, to the nearest km?”

How to solve it:

  1. The side of a square is the square root of its area.
  2. Estimate the square root as in kind 3.
  3. Write the answer with the unit.
Worked example (book). √150 ≈ 12: each side of the search area is about 12 km long.
Exercise 9. √125 ≈ 11: about 11 km.

The core idea: side = √area.

5. Estimate a sum or a difference Exercises 32–37

The question: “Estimate √18 + √9 to the nearest integer.”

How to solve it:

  1. Estimate each square root to a whole number.
  2. Add or subtract the estimates.
  3. For a power, work it out exactly (8² = 64).
Exercise 32. √18 ≈ 4 and √9 = 3, so 4 + 3 = 7.
Exercise 35. √98 ≈ 10 and √89 ≈ 9 gives 1. A calculator and rounding the exact result gives 0, so your teacher may accept either.

The core idea: estimate each root first, then add or subtract.

6. Squares: area and perimeter Exercises 38, 39, 41, 44

The question: “Find the area of a square with perimeter 32 m.”

How to solve it:

  1. A square has four equal sides: side = perimeter ÷ 4.
  2. Area = side × side. If the area is given, the side is its square root.
  3. Perimeter = 4 × side.
Exercise 38. side = 32 ÷ 4 = 8 m, area = 8 × 8 = 64 m².
Exercise 39. side = √49 = 7 m, perimeter = 4 × 7 = 28 m.

The core idea: move between side, area and perimeter.

7. Use a formula; challenge Exercises 40, 47

The question: “D = 3.56 × √A with A = 1 800. Find D.” / “Find √(5² + 12²).”

How to solve it:

  1. Substitute the value into the formula.
  2. Work out the square root with a calculator and round as asked.
  3. Inside a radical sign, work out the sum first, then take the square root.
Exercise 47. 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 and √169 = 13.

The core idea: order of operations still applies.

8. Explain and choose a strategy Think and Discuss 1, 2; exercises 45, 46

The question: “Explain how to estimate √75.”

How to solve it:

  1. Use the method of kind 3 and say it in your own words.
  2. Mention the perfect squares nearest the number.
Think and Discuss 1. 64 < 75 < 81, so 8 < √75 < 9; closer to 81, so √75 ≈ 9.Mine, not from the book

The core idea: say the steps in your own words.

9. Spiral review: evaluate, convert, test prep Exercises 48–55

The question: “Evaluate 38 + 14 + 22 + 36.” / “4 L = ? mL.”

How to solve it:

  1. Evaluate: pair numbers that make tens.
  2. Convert: to a smaller unit multiply, to a larger unit divide.
  3. Scientific notation: the first part is at least 1 and less than 10.
  4. Choices: work out each one.
Exercise 48. 38 + 22 = 60 and 14 + 36 = 50, so 110.
Exercise 54. 2 430 000 = 2.43 × 10⁶ (choice d).

The core idea: use the rules of the earlier lessons.
