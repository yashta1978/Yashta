Key idea

A number in scientific notation has two parts that are multiplied: a number greater than or equal to 1 and less than 10, and a power of 10. The exponent is the number of places the decimal point moves: left to write a large number in scientific notation, right to write it in standard form.

This page explains each kind of question once. Exercises that repeat the same question are explained together, because the method is the same.

1. Vocabulary: the two parts Vocabulary 1; page 16

The question: “In 3.456 × 10⁶, what are the first part and the second part?”

How to solve it:

  1. Read the picture on page 16 (green and purple notes).
  2. The first part is a number greater than or equal to 1 and less than 10.
  3. The second part is a power of 10.
Worked example (book). 3.456 × 10⁶: the first part is 3.456 and the second part is 10⁶.

The core idea: two parts are multiplied: a number from 1 to less than 10, and a power of 10.

2. Write a large number in scientific notation Example 1; exercises 1–6, 13–21, 37–42, 52, 53

The question: “Write 8 296 000 in scientific notation.”

How to solve it:

  1. Move the decimal point to the left to form a number greater than or equal to 1 and less than 10: 8.296.
  2. Count how many places the decimal point moved: 6 places.
  3. Write the number times 10 with that exponent: 8.296 × 10⁶.
  4. Check: the first part is at least 1 and less than 10.
Worked example (book). 700 000 = 7 × 10⁵ and 8 296 000 = 8.296 × 10⁶ and 58 000 = 5.8 × 10⁴.
Exercise 40. 1 000 = 1 × 10³ (the decimal point moves 3 places left).

The core idea: the exponent is the number of places the decimal point moves.

3. Write a number in standard form Example 2; exercises 7–12, 22–36

The question: “Write 3.2 × 10⁷ in standard form.”

How to solve it:

  1. Look at the power of 10: the exponent is 7.
  2. Move the decimal point that number of places to the right.
  3. Write zeros when there are not enough digits.
Worked example (book). 8.753 × 10² = 875.3 and 3.2 × 10⁷ = 32 000 000 and 2.001 × 10¹ = 20.01.
Exercise 27. 8.1164 × 10⁸ = 811 640 000.

The core idea: the exponent says how many places to move the decimal point right.

4. Measurements in scientific notation Exercises 43–48

The question: “Write 4 km = ? m using scientific notation.”

How to solve it:

  1. Convert to the new unit first (lesson 1-3): to a smaller unit, multiply.
  2. Write the converted number in scientific notation.
Exercise 43. 4 km = 4 000 m = 4 × 10³ m.
Exercise 45. 18 L = 18 000 mL = 1.8 × 10⁴ mL.

The core idea: convert first, then write the notation.

5. Word problems with large numbers Exercises 49–53

The question: “About 579 million computers. Write the number in standard form and in scientific notation.”

How to solve it:

  1. Write the number in standard form (1 million = 1 000 000).
  2. Then use the steps of kind 2.
  3. For a pictograph, read the key (1 chair = 25 000 seats), count the chairs and multiply.
  4. For rounding, find the place to round to and look at the next digit.
Exercise 52. 579 million = 579 000 000 = 5.79 × 10⁸.
Exercise 50. 6 chairs × 25 000 = 150 000 = 1.5 × 10⁵.
Exercise 53. 438 317 rounds to 400 000 = 4 × 10⁵.

The core idea: standard form first, then the notation.

6. What’s the error? and explain Exercises 54, 55; Think and Discuss 1–3

The question: “A student said 56 320 000 = 56.32 × 10⁶. Describe the error.” / “Tell why 782.5 × 10⁸ is not correctly written.”

How to solve it:

  1. Check the first part: it must be at least 1 and less than 10.
  2. If it is not, move the decimal point and change the exponent to keep the value the same.
  3. Say it in your own words.
Exercise 54. 56.32 is not less than 10. Correct: 56 320 000 = 5.632 × 10⁷.
Think and Discuss 2. 782.5 × 10⁸ = 7.825 × 10¹⁰.Mine, not from the book

The core idea: the first part must be from 1 to less than 10.

7. Compare numbers; powers; multiply by a power of ten (spiral review) Exercises 57–62

The question: “Compare 4 879 204 and 4 895 190.” / “Use powers to write 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3.” / “30.62 × 10 000.”

How to solve it:

  1. Compare digits from the left: the first different place decides.
  2. Powers: the factor is the base; the number of factors is the exponent.
  3. Multiplying by a power of ten: move the decimal point right as many places as the exponent (zeros).
Exercise 59. 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 3⁵.
Exercise 62. 30.62 × 10 000 = 306 200.

The core idea: use the rules from the earlier lessons.
