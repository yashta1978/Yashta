Key idea

Mental math uses three number properties. Commutative: you can add or multiply in any order. Associative: when you only add or only multiply, you can group the numbers any way. Distributive: a number times a sum can be split: 6 × (10 + 4) = (6 × 10) + (6 × 4).

This page explains each kind of question once. Exercises that repeat the same question are explained together, because the method is the same.

1. Name the property Vocabulary 1–3

The question: “Which property says: you can add or multiply numbers in any order?”

How to solve it:

  1. Read the sentence in the Words column of the three tables.
  2. Match it with the table title: Commutative = ordering, Associative = grouping, Distributive = multiplying a sum.
  3. Type the one word.
Worked example. “Group any of the numbers together” is in the Associative table (title: grouping). Answer: associative.

The core idea: each property has one key word: ordering, grouping, splitting a sum.

2. Evaluate a sum or a product by regrouping Example 1; exercises 1–4, 13–16, 25–30

The question: “Evaluate 13 + 9 + 7 + 11” or “Evaluate 5 × 14 × 4.”

How to solve it:

  1. Look for two numbers whose sum (or product) is a multiple of 10.
  2. Use the Commutative Property to put them next to each other.
  3. Use the Associative Property to put them in a bracket.
  4. Work out the bracket, then finish with mental math.
Worked example (book). 12 + 4 + 18 + 46 → 12 + 18 + 4 + 46 → (12 + 18) + (4 + 46) → 30 + 50 → 80.
Product (exercise 3). 5 × 14 × 4: 5 × 4 = 20, so 14 × (5 × 4) = 14 × 20 = 280.

The core idea: move the numbers so that the easy pairs come first (they make 10, 20, 100 …). The answer never changes.

3. Multiply with the Distributive Property Example 2; exercises 5–12, 17–24, 31–42

The question: “Use the Distributive Property to find 4 × 23.”

How to solve it:

  1. “Break apart” the greater factor into a multiple of 10 and a one-digit number: 23 = 20 + 3.
  2. Write the product as a bracket: 4 × (20 + 3).
  3. Use the Distributive Property: (4 × 20) + (4 × 3).
  4. Multiply in your head: 80 + 12, then add: 92.
Worked example (book). 8 × 74 = 8 × (70 + 4) = (8 × 70) + (8 × 4) = 560 + 32 = 592.
Exercise 18. 4 × 42 = 4 × (40 + 2) = 160 + 8 = 168.

The core idea: a hard multiplication becomes two easy ones (tens and ones). Check: the answer must equal the normal product.

4. A table with a price and a total Exercise 43 (Business)

The question: “Zerkar orders 7 disks. What will his total cost be?” (a table with Number, Unit Cost with Tax, Price, Shipping & Handling, Total.)

How to solve it:

  1. Price = number of disks × unit cost.
  2. Total = Price + Shipping & Handling.
  3. Fill the grey cells in the table.
Worked answer. Price = 7 × 500 = 3 500 Dinars. Total = 3 500 + 1 500 = 5 000 Dinars.

The core idea: read the table cell by cell: each grey cell is one small calculation.

5. Word problems Exercises 44–46

The question: “Four frogs each laid 27 eggs. How many eggs in all?” / “15 vines, 12 hedges, 8 trees and 35 plants: how many?”

How to solve it:

  1. Decide the operation: equal groups mean multiply; putting amounts together means add.
  2. Write the expression (4 × 27, or 15 + 12 + 8 + 35).
  3. Solve it with the property from kind 2 or kind 3.
  4. Write the answer with its unit (eggs, plants, °C).
Exercise 44. 4 × 27 = 4 × (20 + 7) = 80 + 28 = 108 eggs.
Exercise 46. 38 + 4 + 2 + 3 = (38 + 2) + (4 + 3) = 40 + 7 = 47°C.

The core idea: turn the story into one expression, then use the same tricks.

6. What’s the error? Exercise 47

The question: “A student wrote 5 + 24 + 25 + 6 = 5 + 25 + 24 + 6 by the Associative Property. What error did the student make?”

How to solve it:

  1. Compare the two sides number by number.
  2. Ask: did the order change, or only the brackets?
  3. Name the property that matches.
Answer. 24 and 25 swapped places and there are no brackets, so the student used the Commutative Property, not the Associative Property.

The core idea: order = commutative; grouping = associative.

7. Write a problem Exercise 48

The question: “Give a problem you could simplify using the Commutative and Associative Properties. Show the steps and label the properties.”

How to solve it:

  1. Choose numbers that make 10 or 20 when you pair them.
  2. Write a short story or an expression.
  3. Show the order step (Commutative) and the bracket step (Associative), and label each one.
Example. 17 + 8 + 13 + 2 → (Commutative) 17 + 13 + 8 + 2 → (Associative) (17 + 13) + (8 + 2) = 30 + 10 = 40.Mine, not from the book

The core idea: build the problem backwards from easy pairs.

8. Write about it Exercise 49

The question: “Why can you simplify 5(50 + 3) using the Distributive Property? Why can’t you simplify 5(50) + 3?”

How to solve it:

  1. Ask what the 5 multiplies in each expression.
  2. In 5(50 + 3) the 5 multiplies the whole sum.
  3. In 5(50) + 3 the 5 multiplies only 50, and 3 is added afterwards.
Check. 5(50 + 3) = 5 × 53 = 265 = 250 + 15. But 5(50) + 3 = 250 + 3 = 253, so they are different.Mine, not from the book

The core idea: the property needs a number multiplying a sum.

9. Challenge Exercise 50

The question: “Explain how you could find the product of 25 × 112 using the Distributive Property. Evaluate the expression.”

How to solve it:

  1. Break 112 into 100 + 12 (a multiple of 100 is easy).
  2. Write 25 × (100 + 12) = (25 × 100) + (25 × 12).
  3. 2 500 + 300 = 2 800.
Check. 25 × 12 = 300 because 25 × 4 = 100 and 12 = 4 × 3.

The core idea: choose the split that gives the easiest parts.

10. Value of a digit Exercises 51–54

The question: “Write the value of the blue digit in each number.” (The blue digit cannot be seen in this photo.)

How to solve it:

  1. Name the places from the right: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten thousands, hundred thousands.
  2. Find the place of the digit.
  3. Value = digit × place value.
Example. In 649 809 the digit 4 is in the ten thousands place: 4 × 10 000 = 40 000.Mine, not from the book

The core idea: the same digit has a different value in a different place.

11. Word form Exercises 55–58

The question: “Write each number in word form.”

How to solve it:

  1. Split the number into groups of three from the right.
  2. Say each group, then its name (thousand, million).
  3. Use a hyphen between tens and ones: forty-five.
Example. 24 498 = twenty-four thousand, four hundred ninety-eight.

The core idea: read the number out loud group by group.

12. Standard form Exercises 59–60

The question: “Write each number in standard form.”

How to solve it:

  1. Write each group as digits: “two hundred thirty four thousand” → 234.
  2. Fill empty places with zeros.
  3. Join the groups: 234 679.
Example. Fifteen million nine hundred three thousand one hundred eight = 15 903 108 (no tens in “one hundred eight”, so a zero).

The core idea: a missing place always needs a zero.

13. Test prep (place value) Exercise 61

The question: “In which number is the digit 4 in the thousands place?”

How to solve it:

  1. Find the 4 in each choice.
  2. Count its place from the right (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands).
  3. Pick the choice where it is the thousands place.
Answer. b: 14 307 (the 4 is in the thousands place). In a: hundreds; in c: ten thousands; in d: hundred thousands.

The core idea: check every choice before you answer.

14. Think and Discuss Think and Discuss 1–2

The question: “Give an example of the Commutative Property and the Associative Property.” / “Name some situations in which you might use mental math.”

How to solve it:

  1. Look at the Numbers column of the tables on page 4 and write an equation like each.
  2. Think of real situations where you calculate in your head.
Example. 7 + 5 = 5 + 7 and (7 + 5) + 3 = 7 + (5 + 3). Situations: adding prices, finding change.Mine, not from the book

The core idea: give your own example; many answers are correct.
