Key idea

A number is divisible by another number if there is no remainder when you divide (Remember box, page 30). Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8; odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9.

This page explains each kind of question once. Exercises that repeat the same question are explained together, because the method is the same.

1. Write a rule and two numbers Activity, exercises 1–5

The question: “Write a rule for numbers that are divisible by 2. Write two different numbers that are divisible by 2.” Exercises 2, 3, 4 and 5 ask the same for 3, 5, 8 and 9.

How to solve it:

  1. Read the two lists on the page: black numbers are divisible, red numbers are not.
  2. Ask: what do all the black numbers share that no red number has? Look at the last digit (for 2 and 5), the last three digits (for 8) or the sum of the digits (for 3 and 9).
  3. Write that idea as one sentence: “A number is divisible by … if …”.
  4. Choose two different numbers and test both with your rule before you write them.
Worked example (exercise 2, the number 3).
Black: 387 → 3 + 8 + 7 = 18; 426 → 4 + 2 + 6 = 12; 8 004 → 8 + 0 + 0 + 4 = 12; 420 → 4 + 2 + 0 = 6. All sums are in the 3 times table.
Red: 782 → 17; 425 → 11; 1 451 → 11; 332 → 8. None is divisible by 3.
Rule: a number is divisible by 3 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3. Two numbers: 387 and 426.

The core idea: every divisibility rule looks at the part of the number that decides the remainder — the last digit, the last digits, or the sum of the digits.

2. The sieve of Eratosthenes page 31, the table

The question: “Use this method to find all of the prime numbers in the table.”

How to solve it:

  1. Cross out 1.
  2. Circle 2, then cross out all the other numbers divisible by 2.
  3. Circle the next number that is not crossed out (3) and cross out its multiples. Repeat with 5 and 7.
  4. When no new number can be crossed out, circle every number that is left. Circled numbers are prime; crossed-out numbers are composite.
Worked example (the number 9). 9 is a multiple of 3 (3 × 3), so it is crossed out when you circle 3. The number 11 is never crossed out, so it is prime.Mine, not from the book

The core idea: a composite number is a multiple of a smaller prime, so the sieve removes it. What survives has no smaller factor, so it is prime. The sieve finds 25 primes up to 100. Use 👁 on page 31 to watch the table being marked step by step.

3. Are all even numbers divisible by 2? Think and Discuss 1

How to solve it: to say “all” is true, show that the rule works for every number of that kind.

  1. The rule for 2: the last digit is even (0, 2, 4, 6 or 8).
  2. Every even number ends in 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8 — that is what “even” means (Remember box).
Answer: yes — every even number ends in an even digit, so it passes the rule. Example: 248 ends in 8, and 248 = 2 × 124.

4. Are all odd numbers divisible by 3? Think and Discuss 2

How to solve it: to show that “all” is false you need only one example that breaks it (a counterexample).

  1. Pick an odd number, for example 5.
  2. Test it with the rule for 3: the sum of the digits is 5, and 5 is not divisible by 3.
Answer: no. 5 is odd but not divisible by 3. (Some odd numbers are divisible by 3, for example 387 where 3 + 8 + 7 = 18, but not all of them.)

The core idea: “odd” and “divisible by 3” are different properties. One counterexample is enough to answer “not all”.

5. Name a number that satisfies the rule and one that does not Try This, rows 1–8

How to solve it:

  1. Read the rule in the row.
  2. Choose a number you can test quickly and test it with the rule — do not just guess.
  3. For the number that does not satisfy the rule, change one digit of your first number so that the rule breaks, then test again.
Worked example (row 3: divisible by 4 if the last two digits form a number divisible by 4).
8 004 → last two digits 04 = 4 → 4 is divisible by 4 → satisfies.
5 977 → last two digits 77 → 77 ÷ 4 = 19 remainder 1 → does not satisfy.
Row 5 (6): both rules at once. 420 is even (ends in 0) and 4 + 2 + 0 = 6 is divisible by 3, so 420 is divisible by 6. 81 is odd, so it fails the rule for 2 and cannot be divisible by 6.

The core idea: a rule is a quick test. The two numbers show that you can tell the difference between a number that passes the test and one that fails it.

6. A 4-digit number with no repeated digit Exercises 9–14

The question: “Use number tiles to find a number in which no digit is repeated for each description”, for example “4-digit number divisible by 3, 5 and 9”.

How to solve it:

  1. Turn every “divisible by …” into what it needs: by 5 or 10 → the last digit; by 3 or 9 → the sum of the digits; by 4 → the last two digits.
  2. Choose the last digit first. Then choose the other digits so that the sum of all digits is right.
  3. Make sure no digit appears twice and the number has 4 digits.
  4. Test the number by dividing it.
Worked example (exercise 11: divisible by 3, 5 and 9). By 5: the last digit is 0 or 5. By 9: the sum of the digits is 9 or 18. A sum divisible by 9 is also divisible by 3, so 3 is covered. Try 1 035: 1 + 0 + 3 + 5 = 9 and it ends in 5; the digits 1, 0, 3, 5 are all different. Check: 1 035 ÷ 45 = 23.Mine, not from the book
Exercise 12 (divisible by 4 and 9). Try 1 836: the last two digits 36 ÷ 4 = 9, and 1 + 8 + 3 + 6 = 18 is divisible by 9; all digits are different.Mine, not from the book

The core idea: break one hard question into small rules, satisfy them one by one, then check the “no repeated digit” condition last.
