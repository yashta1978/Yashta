Key idea

A numerical expression is made up of numbers and operations. To simplify it, follow the order of operations: first the operations within grouping symbols, then powers, then multiply and divide from left to right, and last add and subtract from left to right.

This page explains each kind of question once. Exercises that repeat the same question are explained together, because the method is the same.

1. Evaluate with +, −, × and ÷ Example 1(a), (b); exercises 1, 2, 8, 9, 39–42

The question: “Evaluate 36 − 18 ÷ 2 × 3 + 8.”

How to solve it:

  1. Multiply and divide from left to right (rule 3).
  2. Then add and subtract from left to right (rule 4).
  3. Write the expression again after each single operation.
Worked example (book). 36 − 18 ÷ 2 × 3 + 8: 18 ÷ 2 = 9, then 9 × 3 = 27, so 36 − 27 + 8 = 9 + 8 = 17.
Exercise 2. 28 − 4 × 3 ÷ 6 + 4: 4 × 3 = 12, then 12 ÷ 6 = 2, so 28 − 2 + 4 = 26 + 4 = 30.

The core idea: multiply and divide first, then add and subtract, each from left to right.

2. Evaluate an expression with a power Example 1(c); exercises 3, 10, 14

The question: “Evaluate 5 + 6² × 10.”

How to solve it:

  1. Evaluate the power first (rule 2).
  2. Multiply and divide from left to right (rule 3).
  3. Add and subtract from left to right (rule 4).
Worked example (book). 5 + 6² × 10 = 5 + 36 × 10 = 5 + 360 = 365.
Exercise 3. 25 − 4² ÷ 8 = 25 − 16 ÷ 8 = 25 − 2 = 23.

The core idea: a power is evaluated before multiplying, dividing, adding or subtracting.

3. Evaluate with grouping symbols Example 2(a), (b); exercises 4–6, 11–13, 15, 16

The question: “Evaluate [(4 + 12 ÷ 4) − 2]³.”

How to solve it:

  1. Find the innermost grouping symbols and work inside them first (rule 1).
  2. Inside them the same rules apply: powers, then multiply and divide, then add and subtract.
  3. When the grouping symbols hold one number, remove them. Then evaluate the power.
Worked example (book). [(4 + 12 ÷ 4) − 2]³ = [(4 + 3) − 2]³ = [7 − 2]³ = 5³ = 125.
Exercise 5. (3² + 11) ÷ 5 = (9 + 11) ÷ 5 = 20 ÷ 5 = 4.

The core idea: start with the innermost grouping symbols.

4. Evaluate the expression in a word problem Example 3; exercises 7, 17, 30

The question: “Evaluate (6 + 5 × 3) × 20 000 to find out how much Dara earned.”

How to solve it:

  1. The expression is given: do not build it yourself.
  2. Follow the rules: parentheses first, then multiply.
  3. Write the answer with the unit (Dinars).
Worked example (book). (6 + 5 × 3) × 20 000 = (6 + 15) × 20 000 = 21 × 20 000 = 420 000 Dinars.
Exercise 7. (3 × 4 + 2 × 8) × 10 000 = (12 + 16) × 10 000 = 28 × 10 000 = 280 000 Dinars.
Exercise 30. 8 000 × 4 + 1 000 × 5 = 32 000 + 5 000 = 37 000. The text says 10 000 Dinars per hour, so the book may mean 10 000 × 5, which gives 82 000. Ask your teacher.

The core idea: the book gives the expression; you only evaluate it.

5. Compare two expressions Exercises 18–23

The question: “Compare 8 × 3 − 2 and 8 × (3 − 2).”

How to solve it:

  1. Find the value of the left side with the order of operations.
  2. Find the value of the right side in the same way.
  3. Write <, > or = between the two values.
Exercise 18. 8 × 3 − 2 = 24 − 2 = 22 and 8 × (3 − 2) = 8 × 1 = 8, so 22 > 8.
Exercise 21. 18 + 6 − 2 = 22 and 18 + (6 − 2) = 22, so they are equal.

The core idea: grouping symbols can change the value, or leave it the same.

6. Insert grouping symbols Exercises 24–29; Think and Discuss 3

The question: “Insert grouping symbols to make 4 × 8 − 3 = 20 true.”

How to solve it:

  1. Find the value without grouping symbols (here 4 × 8 − 3 = 29).
  2. Put parentheses around two numbers so that part is done first.
  3. Check that the value is the one you need.
Exercise 24. 4 × (8 − 3) = 4 × 5 = 20.
Think and Discuss 3. 3 + (9 − 4) × 2 = 3 + 5 × 2 = 3 + 10 = 13.

The core idea: try the parentheses in different places and check the value.

7. Explain the order Think and Discuss 1, 2; exercise 34

The question: “Give the correct order of operations for evaluating (5 + 3 × 20) ÷ 13 + 3².”

How to solve it:

  1. Say what is done first: the grouping symbols.
  2. Inside them: multiply, then add.
  3. Then the power, then the division, and the addition last.
Think and Discuss 2. 3 × 20 = 60, then 5 + 60 = 65; 3² = 9; 65 ÷ 13 = 5; 5 + 9 = 14.
Think and Discuss 1. 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 but (3 + 4)² = 7² = 49, so the values are not the same.

The core idea: describe each operation in the order it is performed.

8. Write an expression from a graph or a picture Exercises 31, 32

The question: “Write and evaluate an expression to find the average temperature over the 4-month period.”

How to solve it:

  1. Read the numbers you need from the graph or the picture.
  2. Put the sum in parentheses, then divide by the number of values.
  3. Write an expression for each part, then one for the total.
Exercise 31(a). The bars are about 27, 24, 28 and 33 °C: (27 + 24 + 28 + 33) ÷ 4 = 112 ÷ 4 = 28 °C. The values are read from the graph, so they are estimates.
Exercise 32(c). 4 × 15 000 + 2 × 30 000 + 6 000 = 60 000 + 60 000 + 6 000 = 126 000 Dinars.

The core idea: number of items × price, then add everything.

9. Choose a strategy; challenge Exercises 33, 35

The question: “Which ages make the sum of the squares of the three youngest equal to the square of the oldest?”

How to solve it:

  1. Test every choice.
  2. Square the three smaller ages and add them.
  3. Compare with the square of the oldest age.
Exercise 33. Choice a: 1² + 4² + 8² = 1 + 16 + 64 = 81 = 9².
Exercise 35 (challenge). 3 + (5 − 6) + 2 × (54 − 5) = 2 + 98 = 100. Other answers are possible.Mine, not from the book

The core idea: test each possibility with the order of operations.

10. Spiral review Exercises 36–39

The question: “Write 402; 398; 417; 410 from least to greatest.” / “Which number is equivalent to 53 × 10⁷?”

How to solve it:

  1. Order numbers: compare the digits from the greatest place.
  2. A power with the exponent 0 is 1.
  3. Multiply by a power of 10: move the decimal point.
Exercise 36. 398; 402; 410; 417.
Exercise 38. 64⁰ = 1, so choice a is not equal to 64.
Exercise 39. 53 × 10⁷ = 530 000 000 (choice d).

The core idea: use the rules of the earlier lessons.

11. Partial Test, lessons 1-1 to 1-5 Page 24, exercises 1–44

The question: “Evaluate 6 × 22.” / “Convert 5 320 m to km.” / “Write 39 500 000 in scientific notation.”

How to solve it:

  1. Lesson 1-1: use easy parts or tens to compute.
  2. Lesson 1-2: a power shows the number of equal factors; 11⁰ = 1.
  3. Lesson 1-3: to a smaller unit multiply by 1 000, to a larger unit divide by 1 000.
  4. Lesson 1-4: the first part is at least 1 and less than 10.
  5. Lesson 1-5: the four rules of the order of operations.
Exercise 4. 6 × 22 = 6 × 20 + 6 × 2 = 120 + 12 = 132.
Exercise 23. 5 320 m = 5 320 ÷ 1 000 = 5.32 km.
Exercise 33. 39 500 000 = 3.95 × 10⁷.
Exercise 44. [6 × (5 + 7) ÷ 9]² = [6 × 12 ÷ 9]² = [72 ÷ 9]² = 8² = 64.

The core idea: each lesson has its own method.
